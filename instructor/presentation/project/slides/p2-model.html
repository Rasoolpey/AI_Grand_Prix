PART 2 · THE MODEL

One mass, a few forces, one grip budget

EVERY 0.02 s

1

Steer  eq. 7

The front wheel turns towards your command: at most 0.5 rad, at most 1.5 rad/s.

2

Push and resist  eqs. 1–4, 9

Speed changes by the force balance: m·dv/dt = F_drive − F_drag − F_roll − F_grade − F_brake.

3

Share the grip  eq. 5

Turning, driving and braking share one budget: √(F_x² + F_y²) ≤ μ·N. Too much turning and the car understeers: it runs wide, with no grip left to brake. Wet or mud: μ × 0.6; mud also rolls 4× harder.

4

Spend energy  eq. 8

E ← E − F_drive·v·dt / 0.85. Braking recovers nothing. An empty battery means no drive: the car coasts.

AI Workshop · Part 2 · the nine equations are in AI_Grand_Prix\PHYSICS.md

The whole simulator is these four steps with the nine short equations in PHYSICS.md. It is deterministic: the same car and driver always give the same run, so a change in the result is caused by a change the student made. The grip budget (eq. 5) is the one idea to land: braking in a corner steals grip from turning, which is why braking before the corner beats braking in it. In the race view, the HUD's friction-circle dot shows this live.
